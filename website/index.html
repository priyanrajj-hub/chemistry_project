The Problem

1

Invisible Degradation

Lithium-ion batteries degrade both electrically and chemically in ways totally invisible to standard voltage checks.

2

Safety Margin Shrink

Internal shorts and charge-transfer issues can build up silently, risking sudden failure.

3

EIS Adds Info

EIS is a proven method to see inside the cell. It injects AC signals to plot complex impedance signatures over varying frequencies.

4

The $10K Barrier

Commercial EIS potentiostats cost between $5,000 and $20,000, making them completely inaccessible for student and low-resource environments.

How It Works

1. Hardware Architecture

  • ESP32: WiFi-enabled brain, directing the frequency sweeps.
  • AD9833 Module: Generates the precise sine-wave excitation signal.
  • ADS1115: 16-bit ADC reading the current and voltage at max 860 Samples Per Second.

2. Budget

Total Estimated Bill of Materials for this setup amounts to roughly ₹2,440. Feasibility rests entirely on executing software logic effectively instead of relying on pure hardware filters.

3. Feature & ML Pipeline

Raw V/I ➝ DFT ➝ Z(f) ➝ Features (R0, Rct) ➝ Random Forest SoH ML

Instead of heavy iterative CNLS fitting over a Randles circuit, we directly extract macro-level features like $R_{ohm\_est}$ and $Phase_{10Hz}$ and pipe them directly into a Random Forest Regressor trained on real NASA degradation data.

Safety & Sustainability

Zero destructive testing. We do not deliberately fail or damage live Li-ion cells. Artificial fault emulation is done via external proxy circuits.

We rely on standard, reusable microcontroller modules and RoHS-leaning components to minimize the toxic footprint, ensuring completely safe deployment in a university lab environment.

Honest Limitations

This is an extreme low-cost apparatus. It cannot match the sub-3% error broadband capabilities of precision desktop analyzers.

The 860 SPS limit makes it exclusively a low-frequency demonstrator. Contact resistance from breadboard wiring (lacking a proper 4-wire Kelvin harness) induces artificial ohmic errors that the ML model attempts to smooth over.
